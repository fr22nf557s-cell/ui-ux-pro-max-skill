Permission to fly

A drone does not become legal to fly because it is well built. UK drone flight is governed by the Air Navigation Order and the CAA's rules made under it, and an autonomous system that patrols a boundary sits in the harder half of those rules: it is the beyond-visual-line-of-sight case, which needs an Operational Authorisation rather than the Open category anyone can fly under.

Airspace & regulation sets out what that means in practice, including the question of who holds the authorisation — the company running the fleet or the householder whose garden it patrols. That question is not settled marketing copy and we will not pretend otherwise; it is being worked through with the CAA's published guidance and, where needed, with advisers.

Data protection

An aircraft with a camera over a residential boundary processes personal data — of the household, of visitors, and of anyone the other side of the fence. Hornet Drones Limited is the controller for the data this website collects, and the privacy notice describes that processing in full: what is collected, why, on what lawful basis, who processes it for us and how long it is kept.

Two things are required before the product itself starts processing footage, and both will be published here:

  • A data protection impact assessment. UK GDPR Article 35 requires one where processing is likely to result in a high risk to people, and the Information Commissioner treats surveillance of this kind as exactly that.
  • Registration with the Information Commissioner's Office, which for most controllers means paying the annual data protection fee.

Product conformity

The aircraft and the charging nest are radio equipment placed on the Great Britain market, which brings them under the Radio Equipment Regulations 2017 and requires UKCA marking supported by a declaration of conformity and a technical file. The declaration will be published here, and supplied with every system.

Voluntary standards

Some certifications are not legally required but are what a serious buyer asks for — information security certification, and the security-industry inspectorate approvals that cover installers working in people's homes. We will say plainly on this page which of these we hold and which we do not. A blank is a blank, not a hint.
